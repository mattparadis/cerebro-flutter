Build a Flutter mobile app called **Rigattiere**, a second-hand marketplace
for a single city. Work autonomously: do not ask me questions, make
reasonable assumptions and list them in your final message.

### Who it is for

People who buy and sell used things locally: students furnishing a room on
a budget, parents passing on kids' gear, collectors hunting for one
specific object. They use the app one-handed, often outdoors or on public
transport, for short sessions. The first thing they want on any screen is
to judge quickly whether an item is worth their time: photo, price,
distance, and whether the seller answers. Trust and speed matter more than
features.

### Screens and states

1. **Browse** (`/`): a vertically scrolling feed of items, each with photo,
   title, price, distance and seller first name. A category filter row at
   the top (Furniture, Kids, Bikes, Books, Electronics, Other) and a
   search field. States: loading (skeleton, same layout), full, empty for
   a filter with no results (with a way out), network error with retry.
2. **Item detail** (`/items/:id`): photo carousel, title, price, condition,
   description, distance, seller card (name, response time, rating), and a
   single primary action "Make an offer" that opens the chat. States:
   loaded, loading, item no longer available (sold), not found.
3. **Chat with the seller** (`/chat/:conversationId`): message thread with
   the item pinned at the top (photo, title, price), text input, and an
   **offer** affordance: the buyer proposes a price; the offer appears in
   the thread as a distinct message with Accept / Decline on the seller's
   side and a status on the buyer's side (pending, accepted, declined,
   expired). States: empty conversation (no messages yet), active thread,
   offer pending, offer accepted, offer declined, send failed with retry.
4. **Profile** (`/profile`): the user's avatar and name, rating, member
   since, two tabs or sections "My listings" and "Saved", settings entry
   (notifications, dark mode preference, log out). States: with listings,
   with no listings (first-time seller), loading.
5. **Post an item** (`/sell`, opened from a primary action on Browse):
   up to 5 photos (use placeholders), title, category, condition, price,
   description, pickup area. Validation with clear inline errors. States:
   empty, invalid (show errors), submitting, submit failed with retry,
   success (land on the new item's detail, item visible in "My listings").

Design every state, not just handle it. Empty states explain what to do
next. Errors say what happened in plain words and offer a retry.

### The offer flow has a contract

- Making an offer: the input validates (positive number, not above the
  asking price by more than 10%), the send button is disabled while the
  request is in flight (no double offer), a failed send keeps the typed
  amount and offers retry, a successful send shows the offer message as
  pending immediately.
- The seller side (simulate it in the fake data layer with a delayed
  response): an accepted offer updates the pinned item area with the agreed
  price and a next step; a declined one lets the buyer offer again; after
  a timeout the offer expires visibly.
- Leaving the chat and coming back keeps the thread and the offer state.
- Going back from the detail to Browse keeps the scroll position and the
  selected filter.

Write widget tests for this flow against the fake data layer: offer
persisted, double tap blocked while in flight, failure then retry
succeeds without a duplicate, accept updates the item area. State
clearly in the final message what the tests prove and what would still
need a real backend or device.

### Aesthetic direction

Not a generic marketplace. The feel is a **neighbourhood flea market**:
handwritten price tags, stamped paper, things with a past. Three words:
*warm, honest, a bit cheeky*.

- References: the price-tag typography of old hardware stores (take the
  confident, slightly oversized numerals), and printed classifieds (take
  the dense but orderly rhythm, the ruled separators, the restraint in
  colour). Do not imitate either literally.
- Typography: a display face with character for prices and screen titles,
  a quiet, highly legible sans for everything else. Prices are the
  loudest element on every card.
- Colour: an off-white paper base in light mode, a deep warm charcoal in
  dark mode; one earthy accent (think terracotta, ochre or olive, choose
  one) used only for the primary action and the offer state; status
  colours stay muted. Black ink for text, never pure black on pure white.
- Surfaces: flat paper, hairline rules and generous whitespace instead of
  floating white cards with shadows; corners small, not pill-shaped
  everywhere. A single tactile detail is welcome (a stamp-like badge for
  "sold" or "accepted"), not a texture on every surface.
- Motion: short, physical, used for state changes (offer sent, accepted),
  not for decoration.
- Avoid: gradients, purple or electric blue, rounded-everything, emoji as
  icons, centred layouts on list screens, placeholder-only form fields.

The direction must survive dark mode, large text (1.3×) and real content
(long titles, four-digit prices, a 2-line description cut with an
ellipsis).

### Technical constraints

- Flutter stable, Material 3 as the base, **go_router** for navigation
  with the paths above (nested routes where it makes sense so back works
  from a deep link).
- No backend: an in-memory fake data layer with realistic seed data
  (at least 12 items with varied titles and prices, 3 conversations) and
  simulated latency and failures that tests and screenshots can trigger
  (a query parameter such as `?state=empty|error` on list screens, or
  named fixtures in the test layer; say which).
- Light and dark theme from one source of truth; no hardcoded colours
  in screens; typography through the theme.
- `flutter analyze` clean; all tests pass.

### Deliver

- The project in this folder, runnable with `flutter run`.
- A short `DESIGN.md`: the assumptions you made, how the aesthetic
  direction was translated into the theme (fonts, colours, shapes,
  spacing), the states each screen has and how to reach them, what the
  tests prove and what they do not.
- In the final message: files created, how to reach every state, and the
  three design decisions you would want a reviewer to look at first.
